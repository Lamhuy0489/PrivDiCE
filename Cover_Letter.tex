\opening{Dear Editor,}

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled
\textbf{``PrivDiCE: Privacy-Preserving Actionable Counterfactual Explanations
with Differential Privacy and Homomorphic Encryption''}
for consideration for publication in \textit{Knowledge-Based Systems}.

Counterfactual explanations are increasingly used to provide actionable
information about machine learning decisions, but their generation can create
privacy risks at multiple stages. Existing approaches typically address
counterfactual quality, training-data privacy, or secure inference separately.
In this work, we propose \textbf{PrivDiCE}, a unified framework that explicitly
addresses two complementary privacy surfaces. Differential privacy is used to
limit the influence of sensitive training records on the released
counterfactual generator, while CKKS homomorphic encryption protects client
records and candidate counterfactuals during remote model evaluation.

Beyond integrating these privacy mechanisms, PrivDiCE introduces a semantic
sparse and diverse search procedure that preserves immutable attributes,
enforces feature and group constraints, and selects multiple feasible
counterfactual explanations under controlled candidate budgets. The framework
therefore considers privacy together with actionability, sparsity, diversity,
robustness, and computational cost rather than treating privacy as an
independent post-processing step.

We conduct a bidirectional evaluation on Leipzig ECG, Heart+, and MIMIC-IV.
The study compares PrivDiCE with random, genetic, gradient-based, Wachter-style,
CounterGAN, and official DiCE baselines. It further evaluates empirical privacy
through membership inference, memorization, attribute inference, and
explanation-linkage analyses. Encrypted inference is implemented using real
TenSEAL CKKS execution and evaluated in terms of numerical agreement,
latency, communication overhead, memory use, and ciphertext packing.

The experiments show that PrivDiCE can provide highly sparse and feasible
counterfactual explanations while maintaining strong counterfactual
availability on Leipzig ECG and MIMIC-IV. Heart+ reveals a more challenging
and direction-dependent privacy--utility tradeoff. At the primary privacy
setting, membership-inference performance remains close to random
discrimination, while the broader privacy analysis identifies residual risks
that are not captured by membership inference alone. CKKS evaluation achieves
complete label agreement with plaintext inference on the evaluated candidate
cohorts, although homomorphic model evaluation remains the principal
computational bottleneck.

We believe the manuscript is well aligned with the scope of
\textit{Knowledge-Based Systems} because it contributes a methodological
framework for trustworthy and explainable machine learning, with particular
emphasis on actionable decision support, privacy-preserving knowledge
extraction, and secure model deployment. The work also provides a
multi-objective evaluation protocol that may be useful for the broader study
of privacy-preserving explainable AI systems.

We confirm that this manuscript is original, has not been published previously,
and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. All authors have
approved the manuscript and agree with its submission to
\textit{Knowledge-Based Systems}. The authors declare no competing interests.

Thank you for considering our manuscript. We appreciate your time and look
forward to your response.
